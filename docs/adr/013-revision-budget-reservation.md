# ADR-013: Reserve before revision identity work

The real-Core reproduction in `reports/revision-budget-gap-v1` showed that a revision with only one remaining tool call performed Core identity inspection before rejecting the operation for its finalization reserve. The original ledger did not include that inspection. A failed identity check could likewise leave no charged revision.

Revision preflight now checks the saved records and available allowance without invoking Core. A denied operation creates no revision target. An accepted attempt saves its TaskSpec, registry, manifest and inherited ledger, then records a bound `pending-revision.json` pointer and reserves `resume_identity_check` before Core artifact/report inspection. That reservation also covers evidence copying and the preceding host checks. Core subprocess timeouts share the remaining identity-check allowance instead of resetting it for each report. Local file hashing/copying remains host work rather than a hard OS wall-time sandbox; elapsed overruns are recorded and further operations remain budget-gated.

Exceptions preserve the failed attempt and conservatively settle its pending reservation. A hard interruption leaves a pending reservation. Before accepting another answer or review on the same anchor, the locked host recovers that exact attempt, preserves its costs, quarantines unaccepted evidence, and advances the latest-revision pointer to a PARTIAL report. It never replays Core to finish the abandoned attempt. A repeated payload returns that report; a different payload inherits the consumed budget and can be rejected. An exhausted recovery allowance preserves raw files and remains an error, rather than restarting from the cheaper original ledger.

Successful answers and reviews retain their existing behavior, original reports remain immutable, and image-audit counts do not increase. Recovery of an interrupted revision records its path/kind and explicitly says that this revision accepted no image evidence. The attempt's implementation identity and recovery processor identity are separate. These are local host guarantees tested with real Core and controlled failures; they are not real model or human experiments.

The earlier finalization accounting limitation is not resolved by this change: report publication includes a second render/write after the timed render, and some revision assembly falls outside its reserved phases. This remains an explicit delivery-audit follow-up, not a claim that every host I/O byte is now measured or hard-time-limited.
